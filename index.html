Warband

A playtest build. Two players, one screen, about twenty minutes.

A hex-map miniatures wargame with one twist that shapes everything else: you draft your warband with the battlefield already in front of you. Composition is a decision about terrain, not a deckbuilding exercise done in the dark.

And when a unit dies it permanently reshapes the hex it fell on — a wood becomes a graveyard, a ruin becomes rubble. The ground you fought over is different for the rest of the match. That mechanic is called Last Stand.

Hotseat. Two people share one screen and pass it back and forth. Nothing to install, no account, no sign-in — it all runs in this page. Set the two names, press deal, and go.

What I'd love you to notice

Balance has been simulated across tens of thousands of games. Fun has been tested by nobody — which is the entire reason I'm asking. Blunt answers are more useful than kind ones.

  • Did the visible map actually change what you drafted? That's the central bet of the design. If you found yourself picking on raw unit quality and ignoring the terrain, that is the single most useful thing you could tell me.
  • Ten turns — right, short, or long? If a game ended on the clock rather than with someone breaking, did that ending feel earned or arbitrary?
  • How did the last two or three turns feel? Specifically: when you were ahead, did you want to avoid contact and run down the clock? I suspect that's possible and I'd rather hear it from a player than from a simulation.
  • Did Last Stand ever change your plan? Did you fight for a hex because of what dying there would leave behind — or did the terrain changes just wash over you?
  • Anything you had to be told rather than could see. Every one of those is a bug in the interface, not in you.

Known rough edges

  • Hotseat only. Networked play exists in the code but is switched off at the server end.
  • No AI opponent yet — you need a second human.
  • It's a prototype. Expect unpolished corners; tell me about the ones that got in your way.
